Affine and Non-Affine Deformation of a 2D Lattice

Type the displacement (u, v) of every point as a function of position, or choose a preset, and see how a lattice, a square and a circle deform. When the gradient of the displacement is the same everywhere the deformation is affine; when it varies from place to place it is non-affine.

Instructions for use
  • Affine preset: loads a stretch, a shear or a rotation, in which the displacement is a linear function of position.
  • Non-affine preset: loads a quadratic bend, a swirl, a radial bulge or a sine wave, in which the displacement is not linear in position.
  • u and v boxes: edit the displacement components yourself. You can use the variables X, Y, L (a fixed half-width of 250), k1 and k2; the functions sin, cos, tan, asin, acos, atan, atan2, sqrt, abs, exp, log, log10, pow, min, max, floor, ceil, round; the constants pi and e; and ^ for powers. The formatted equation and the four displacement gradients are shown under the boxes.
  • k1, k2 sliders: two parameters that you can use in the equations, from −1 to 1. Reset k1, k2 sets both to zero.
  • Lattice, square and circle: grey is the original, white is the deformed shape. For the square and circle, n points on the perimeter are moved by the same field and reconnected. Use Boundary points and Shape size to change them, and un-tick the shapes box to hide them.

The lattice shows the raw displacement field. For the square and the circle, n points are sampled along the perimeter, each is moved by the same (u, v) field, and they are reconnected in order, which shows how the point-by-point deformation changes the shape of a whole object.

Physics of the problem

A deformation is described by the displacement \(\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{X})=(u,v)\) of each point from its original position \(\mathbf{X}=(X,Y)\). The local deformation is measured by the displacement gradient:

\( e_{ij}=\dfrac{\partial u_i}{\partial X_j} = \begin{pmatrix} \partial u/\partial X & \partial u/\partial Y\\ \partial v/\partial X & \partial v/\partial Y \end{pmatrix} \)

Affine deformation. If the displacement is a linear function of position, the gradient has the same value at every point. Straight lines stay straight, parallel lines stay parallel, and every part of the body, however small, is deformed in the same way as the whole. The stretches, shears and rotation in the first menu are of this kind. The stretching and shearing is the symmetric part \(\varepsilon_{ij}=\tfrac12(e_{ij}+e_{ji})\) of the gradient, and the rigid rotation is its antisymmetric part.

Non-affine deformation. If the displacement is not linear, the gradient depends on position. Lattice lines bend, and the neighbourhood of one point is deformed differently from that of another. The square and circle show how this alters the overall shape of an object: a square becomes curved, and a circle is no longer an ellipse. (An affine map takes a circle to an ellipse and a square to a parallelogram.)

In a crystal, the elastic deformation of a perfect lattice is affine, to a good approximation. In an amorphous solid or a defective crystal, the atoms do not follow the macroscopic strain exactly, and each atom has an extra, non-affine, displacement that relaxes the local forces. These non-affine displacements are important in the elasticity and the plastic flow of glasses, and in the elastic moduli of materials with a complicated unit cell. The displacement fields here are a simple way of seeing how a displacement that is not a linear function of position distorts an arrangement of atoms.

The gradient shown in the four boxes is calculated symbolically from the equations typed in, so you can read off where it is constant (affine) and where it depends on position.

Glossary
Displacement, (u, v)
How far a point moves from its original position.
Displacement gradient, eij
The derivatives of the displacement components with respect to position.
Affine deformation
A deformation with a displacement gradient that is the same everywhere.
Non-affine deformation
A deformation with a displacement gradient that varies with position.
k1, k2
Free parameters that can be used in the displacement equations.
L
The half-width of the region shown, fixed at 250 units.
