Free Volume in Amorphous and Crystalline Packings

A two-dimensional model: the Voronoi cell of each atom, minus the area of the atom, is its free volume. Compare a random (amorphous) arrangement with square and hexagonal crystals.

Instructions for use
  • Structure: choose Amorphous, Square or Hexagonal, then press Generate. Each press of Generate on Amorphous makes a new random packing.
  • Average distance: sets the mean spacing of the atoms in the amorphous packing (a larger value means fewer atoms and more free volume). It does not affect the two crystals.
  • Show Voronoi cells: colours each cell from blue (smallest cell) to red (largest cell).
  • Histogram: the distribution of free volume per atom. A crystal gives a single spike; the amorphous packing gives a broad distribution.
  • The atomic density N/A and the number of atoms are printed below the plots.

Physics of the problem

In a crystal every atom has the same environment, so every atom has the same share of space. In a glass the atoms are packed at random, so some atoms have more room than others. The Voronoi cell of an atom is the region of space that is closer to that atom than to any other, and it divides the whole volume (here, the area) among the atoms with no gaps. The free volume of an atom is the part of its cell that its own hard core does not occupy:

\( v_f = A_\text{Voronoi} - \pi r^2 \)

where \(r\) is the radius of the atom (fixed at 15 pixel units here). The sum of all Voronoi areas is the area of the box, so the average free volume is set by the density alone; what differs between structures is the spread. A hexagonal arrangement is the densest packing of discs in two dimensions, and its cells are regular hexagons with the smallest possible area, \(2\sqrt{3}\,r^2\).

The spread of free volume matters because atoms in the larger cells can move more easily. In theories of the glass transition and of viscous flow (the free-volume models of Cohen and Turnbull, and of Spaepen), plastic flow in a metallic glass takes place by atomic jumps into regions where the local free volume exceeds a critical value. The tail of the histogram is the population of such sites.

The cells are computed on a periodic box by tiling the packing 5×5 times, so atoms at the edge are not given artificially large cells.

Glossary
Amorphous
A structure with no long-range order.
Voronoi cell
The set of points closer to a given atom than to any other atom.
Free volume
The area (in 2D) of the Voronoi cell not occupied by the atom itself.
Periodic boundary
The box is repeated in all directions, so an atom leaving one side re-enters from the opposite side.
Atomic density
Number of atoms per unit area, N/A.
