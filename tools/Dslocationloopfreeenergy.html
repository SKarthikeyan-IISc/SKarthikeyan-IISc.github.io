Free Energy of a Dislocation Loop: Nucleation under Stress

A loop of radius r costs line energy and gains work from the applied stress. Their sum has a maximum, the energy barrier to nucleating a loop.

Instructions for use
  • τ slider: sets the applied shear stress, in units of the shear modulus μ, from 0 to 0.1.
  • Curves: the red dotted line is the energy penalty E(r) of the dislocation line, the green dotted line is the work W(r) done by the stress, and the blue line is their sum, the free energy F(r).
  • Blue marker: the maximum of F, which is the critical radius r* and the nucleation barrier F*, printed next to it. Loops smaller than r* shrink; loops larger than r* grow freely.
  • Try τ = 0.01, 0.02 and 0.05. With τ = 0 there is no maximum, and every loop shrinks.

  • The applied shear stress τ is in units of μ.
Physics of the problem

Consider a circular dislocation loop of radius \(r\) and Burgers vector \(b\) nucleating on a glide plane under a shear stress \(\tau\). Two terms control its energy.

First, the dislocation line has a line energy of about \(\mu b^2\) per unit length (a typical value), so the energy of the loop, which grows with the length of the line, is

\( E(r) = 2\pi r\cdot \mu b^2 \;\;\rightarrow\;\; 2\pi r \) in units of \(\mu b^3\)

Second, when the loop expands, the part of the glide plane inside it (area \(\pi r^2\)) is displaced by \(b\) and the stress does work \(\tau b\,\pi r^2\). This lowers the free energy:

\( W(r) = -\tau\,\pi r^2 \)

The free energy of the loop is the sum:

\( F(r) = 2\pi r - \tau\pi r^2 \)

At small \(r\) the linear term wins, so \(F\) rises. At large \(r\) the quadratic term wins, so \(F\) falls. The maximum is at

\( r^* = \dfrac{1}{\tau}, \qquad F^* = \dfrac{\pi}{\tau} \)

(in units of \(b\) and \(\mu b^3\)). A loop smaller than \(r^*\) lowers its energy by shrinking, one larger than \(r^*\) by growing. To nucleate a loop, thermal activation (or a stress concentration) must supply the energy \(F^*\), which is why homogeneous nucleation of dislocations needs a stress near the theoretical strength, whereas heterogeneous sources, such as the Frank–Read source, need only a small stress. The same shape of curve, an energy barrier that falls as the driving force rises, arises for the nucleation of a crystal in a liquid.

Glossary
Dislocation loop
A closed dislocation line enclosing a region of slipped crystal.
b
Burgers vector, the magnitude of the slip.
μ
Shear modulus.
τ
Applied resolved shear stress, here in units of μ.
Critical radius, r*
The radius of the loop at the maximum of F(r), above which the loop grows spontaneously.
Nucleation barrier, F*
The free energy of the critical loop.
