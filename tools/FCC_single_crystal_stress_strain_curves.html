Stress–Strain Curves of FCC Single Crystals

Tensile curves of an FCC single crystal loaded along different axes: anisotropic elasticity, single-slip easy glide with lattice rotation (Stage I), and multiple-slip hardening (Stage II).

Instructions for use
  • Material: choose Cu, Al, Ni or Au to load their elastic constants C11, C12 and C44, or type your own values.
  • CRSS τ0 and hardening slope θ0: the critical resolved shear stress for slip (MPa) and the hardening rate used after the crystal enters multiple slip (GPa).
  • Loading orientations: tick any of the preset tensile axes to draw their curves. Type u v w in the orange box to define your own axis, and tick Custom.
  • Max applied strain: the slider sets the end of the curves. Press Recalculate and plot after changing it or any of the numbers.
  • Table: for each orientation it gives Young's modulus along the axis, the initial yield stress, whether the crystal starts in single or multiple slip, and the strain at which easy glide ends (Stage I to Stage II).
  • Compare [001] and [111] (several systems at once, no easy glide) with [123] and [112] (a single system, so a long easy-glide stage).

Physics of the problem

Elasticity. A cubic crystal is elastically anisotropic. Its Young's modulus along a direction with direction cosines \((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\) is

\( \dfrac{1}{E} = S_{11} - 2\left(S_{11}-S_{12}-\tfrac12 S_{44}\right)\left(\alpha^2\beta^2+\beta^2\gamma^2+\gamma^2\alpha^2\right) \)

where the compliances \(S_{ij}\) are obtained from the stiffnesses \(C_{11}, C_{12}, C_{44}\). The modulus is largest along \(\langle111\rangle\) and smallest along \(\langle001\rangle\) when the anisotropy ratio \(2C_{44}/(C_{11}-C_{12})\) is greater than 1, as in most FCC metals (Cu, Ni, Au).

Yielding. Slip begins on the system with the highest Schmid factor \(m\) when the resolved shear stress reaches \(\tau_0\), so the yield stress is \(\sigma_y=\tau_0/m\). Stress is then \(\sigma = \tau_0/m\) while a single system operates, since slip proceeds at constant resolved shear stress (easy glide, Stage I).

Lattice rotation. Slip by \(d\gamma\) on the primary system changes the direction of the tensile axis \(\mathbf{t}\) in the crystal. In tension the axis turns towards the slip direction \(\mathbf{s}\):

\( d\mathbf{t} = d\gamma\,(\mathbf{t}\cdot\mathbf{n})\,\big[\mathbf{s} - (\mathbf{t}\cdot\mathbf{s})\,\mathbf{t}\big], \qquad d\varepsilon = m\,d\gamma \)

so \(m\) changes as the crystal strains. When the axis reaches the edge of the standard stereographic triangle, a second system gets the same Schmid factor as the primary one (the conjugate system, or the critical system), and slip on two or more systems begins. This is the end of Stage I. Slip systems then interact and harden the crystal at a rate that is a fraction of the shear modulus (Stage II); the tool applies a linear hardening law \(\sigma = \sigma_\text{trans} + m\,\theta_0\,(\varepsilon-\varepsilon_\text{trans})\) beyond that point.

Orientations at the corners of the triangle ([001], [011], [111]) and along its [001]–[111] edge (such as [112]) start in multiple slip and have no easy glide. Orientations in the interior of the triangle, such as [123], have a single operating system and a long Stage I. This is why the curves of single crystals depend so strongly on orientation, whereas the curve of a polycrystal is the average over all orientations (the Taylor factor).

This is a simplified model. It neglects elastic strain during plastic flow, latent hardening, the sign of the shear stress, and the constraints of the grips on the rotation of a real specimen.

Glossary
Easy glide (Stage I)
Slip on a single system, with low hardening.
Stage II
Multiple slip, with a high, nearly constant work-hardening rate.
CRSS, τ0
Critical resolved shear stress, at which a slip system operates.
Schmid factor, m
cosφ·cosλ, the ratio of resolved shear stress to applied tensile stress.
Lattice rotation
The change of the crystal orientation relative to the tensile axis caused by slip.
C11, C12, C44
The three independent elastic stiffnesses of a cubic crystal.
θ0
Work-hardening slope used in Stage II.
