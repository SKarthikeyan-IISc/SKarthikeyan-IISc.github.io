Free Energy versus Vacancy Concentration

The enthalpy of vacancies rises linearly with their concentration, the configurational entropy term falls steeply, and the free energy has a minimum at the equilibrium vacancy concentration.

Instructions for use
  • T slider: sets the temperature from 300 to 1000 K.
  • ΔHv slider: sets the enthalpy of formation of a vacancy, in eV per vacancy, from 0 to 1.
  • Curves: red is the enthalpy ΔH(Xv), green is the entropy term −TΔS, and blue is the free energy ΔG, their sum. The blue marker is the minimum of ΔG, labelled with the equilibrium vacancy fraction.
  • Log scale box: plots Xv on a logarithmic axis, from 10−6 to 10−3, which shows the shape of the curves at very low concentrations.
  • If the minimum is not within the range of the plot, no marker is shown.

Physics of the problem

Each vacancy costs an enthalpy \(\Delta H_v\), because bonds are broken. For a vacancy fraction \(X_v\) the enthalpy per atom is therefore

\( \Delta H = X_v\,\Delta H_v \)

The vacancies can be spread over the lattice sites in many ways. The ideal configurational entropy per atom is

\( \Delta S = -k_B\big[X_v\ln X_v + (1-X_v)\ln(1-X_v)\big] \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; -T\Delta S = k_BT\big[X_v\ln X_v + (1-X_v)\ln(1-X_v)\big] \)

Only the configurational entropy is included here, not the vibrational entropy of the atoms near the vacancy. The free energy of the crystal relative to the perfect crystal is \(\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S\). Setting \(d\Delta G/dX_v=0\):

\( \Delta H_v + k_BT\ln\dfrac{X_v}{1-X_v} = 0 \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; X_v^{eq} = \dfrac{1}{1+e^{\Delta H_v/k_BT}} \approx e^{-\Delta H_v/k_BT} \)

The \(X\ln X\) term has an infinite slope at \(X_v = 0\), so a few vacancies always lower the free energy, however large \(\Delta H_v\) is. The minimum moves to higher \(X_v\) and gets deeper as the temperature rises, and moves to lower \(X_v\) as \(\Delta H_v\) increases. Because \(\Delta H_v\) appears in an exponent, a change of a few tenths of an eV changes the equilibrium concentration at a given temperature by several orders of magnitude. (Formation enthalpies of metals are typically 0.5 to 2 eV.)

The energies are in eV per atom, and \(k_B = 8.617\times10^{-5}\) eV/K.

Glossary
Xv
Fraction of lattice sites that are vacant.
ΔHv
Enthalpy of formation of one vacancy (eV).
ΔH, ΔS, ΔG
The enthalpy, entropy and Gibbs free energy of the crystal with vacancies, relative to the perfect crystal, per atom.
Configurational entropy
Entropy from the number of ways of arranging the vacancies on the lattice.
kB
Boltzmann constant.
Equilibrium vacancy fraction
The value of Xv that minimises ΔG.
