Lennard-Jones Potential Explorer

How the interatomic potential between two atoms depends on ε and σ, and how the bottom of the well is approximated by a parabola.

Instructions for use
  • Well depth (ε): the ε slider sets the energy scale, that is, how strongly the two atoms bind.
  • Atomic diameter (σ): the σ slider shifts the distance at which the potential crosses zero, and with it the equilibrium bond length.
  • Harmonic fit: the red dashed parabola touches the potential at its minimum (r0). Its curvature is the stiffness k, shown in the panel on the right.
  • Curvature plots: tick Show curvature plots to see how k varies with ε and with σ.

Taylor expansion about \(r_0\):
\( V(r) = V_0 + V'_0(r-r_0) + \tfrac{1}{2}V''_0(r-r_0)^2 + \dots \)
Since \(V'(r_0) = 0\), truncating at second order gives
\( V(r) \approx V(r_0) + \tfrac{1}{2}k(r - r_0)^2 \)
where the curvature is
\( k = V''(r_0) = \dfrac{72\epsilon}{r_0^2} \)
Physics of the problem

Two neutral atoms attract each other at long range, through fluctuating dipoles (van der Waals attraction, varying as \(r^{-6}\)), and repel each other at short range because their electron clouds cannot overlap (Pauli repulsion). The Lennard-Jones potential models both with two terms:

\( V(r) = 4\epsilon\left[\left(\dfrac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{12} - \left(\dfrac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{6}\right] \)

The \(r^{-12}\) form of the repulsion is chosen for convenience, since it is the square of the attractive term. It is not derived from first principles.

The force is \(F = -dV/dr\). It vanishes at the minimum of the potential, which gives the equilibrium separation and the binding energy:

\( r_0 = 2^{1/6}\sigma \approx 1.122\,\sigma, \qquad V(r_0) = -\epsilon \)

Close to \(r_0\) the potential is nearly a parabola, so the bond behaves like a linear spring of stiffness \(k = V''(r_0) = 72\epsilon/r_0^2\). This is the origin of Hooke's law and of the elastic modulus: stiffer bonds, from a deeper well or a smaller atom, give a stiffer solid. The parabola is symmetric, but the real potential is not. It rises steeply on the compressive side and flattens on the tensile side, which is the origin of thermal expansion. The harmonic approximation is good only for small displacements about \(r_0\).

Glossary
ε
Depth of the potential well. The energy needed to separate the pair from the equilibrium distance to infinity.
σ
Distance at which the potential is zero. It sets the effective size of the atom.
r0
Equilibrium separation, the position of the minimum of V(r).
V(r0)
Value of the potential at the minimum, equal to −ε.
k
Curvature of the potential at the minimum, V″(r0). It is the bond stiffness in the harmonic approximation.
Harmonic approximation
Replacing the potential near its minimum by a parabola, the first non-trivial term of a Taylor series.
