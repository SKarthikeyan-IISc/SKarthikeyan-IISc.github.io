Rayleigh-Plateau Instability: Cylinder Breaking into Spheres

Does a long cylinder lower its surface area by breaking into equal spheres of the same total volume? It depends on how slender the cylinder is, and on how many spheres form.

Instructions for use
  • Break up cylinder: tick the box to show the spheres. Un-tick it to see only the dashed outline of the cylinder.
  • N slider: each of the three cases has its own slider for the number of equal spheres, N = 1 to 8. The total volume is always that of the cylinder.
  • Plot on the right: the total area of the spheres divided by the area of the cylinder, against N, on a logarithmic scale. The thick horizontal line is a ratio of 1. Below it, breaking up lowers the surface area (and so the surface energy); above it, it raises it. The red dot marks the current N.

Physics of the problem

Surface energy is proportional to surface area, so a body of fixed volume lowers its energy by lowering its area. Take a cylinder of radius \(R\) and length \(L\), and divide the same volume into \(N\) equal spheres of radius \(r\):

\( \pi R^2 L = N\,\tfrac43\pi r^3 \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; r = \left(\dfrac{3R^2L}{4N}\right)^{1/3} \)

The ratio of the area of the spheres to that of the curved surface of the cylinder is

\( \dfrac{A_\text{spheres}}{A_\text{cyl}} = \dfrac{4\pi N r^2}{2\pi R L} = \dfrac{2Nr^2}{RL} = 1.65\,N^{1/3}\left(\dfrac{R}{L}\right)^{1/3} \)

so the area of the spheres grows with the number of spheres as \(N^{1/3}\), and the more slender the cylinder (large \(L/R\)), the more it gains by breaking into a few big spheres. For a single sphere the ratio is below 1 when \(L/R > 4.5\) or so, that is, when the cylinder is longer than its diameter times about 2.25. (End caps of the cylinder are ignored.)

The more careful result, due to Plateau and Rayleigh, is that a long liquid cylinder is unstable to a sinusoidal ripple along its axis if the wavelength exceeds the circumference, \(\lambda > 2\pi R\). Ripples of a certain wavelength grow fastest, and the cylinder pinches off into drops separated by about \(9R\). The same instability breaks up a stream of water from a tap, and it is why thin metal wires, nanowires and rod-like precipitates break into rows of particles when heated, by surface diffusion, even though they are solid.

Glossary
R, L
Radius and length of the cylinder. L/R is its aspect ratio.
N
Number of equal spheres into which the cylinder is divided.
Surface energy
Energy per unit area of surface, so total surface energy is proportional to area at fixed material.
Plateau–Rayleigh instability
The instability of a long cylinder (jet, wire) to ripples with a wavelength greater than the circumference.
Area ratio
Surface area of the spheres divided by the surface area of the cylinder; below 1 means the break-up lowers the energy.
