Physics of the problem

Plastic flow in a crystal takes place by slip: dislocations glide on a slip plane (normal \(\mathbf{n}\)) in a slip direction (\(\mathbf{d}\)). Only the shear stress on that plane, in that direction, drives the glide. For a uniaxial tensile stress \(\sigma\) along the axis \(\mathbf{t}\), this resolved shear stress is (Schmid's law):

\( \tau_\text{RSS} = \sigma\cos\phi\cos\lambda = m\,\sigma, \qquad m = (\mathbf{t}\cdot\mathbf{n})(\mathbf{t}\cdot\mathbf{d}) \)

where \(\phi\) is the angle between the tensile axis and the plane normal, \(\lambda\) is the angle between the tensile axis and the slip direction, and \(m\) is the Schmid factor. Its maximum value is 0.5, when both angles are 45°. A system operates when the resolved shear stress reaches the critical resolved shear stress, \(\tau_\text{RSS} \ge \tau_\text{CRSS}\), which is a property of the material, the temperature and the strain rate, but not of the orientation.

In BCC crystals the close-packed directions are \(\langle 111\rangle\), but there is no close-packed plane. Slip occurs on the \(\{110\}\), \(\{112\}\) and sometimes \(\{123\}\) planes, and the critical resolved shear stress is similar on each, so the active plane depends on the orientation, the temperature and the purity. The calculation here covers the 12 \(\{110\}\langle 111\rangle\) and the 12 \(\{112\}\langle 111\rangle\) systems, with a separate CRSS for each family. The systems with the largest Schmid factor reach the CRSS first; they are the primary systems.

Two simplifications are made. The absolute value of each factor is used, so that tension and compression are the same. In a real BCC crystal the screw dislocation core is not planar and the CRSS depends on the sign of the stress and on the stress components other than the resolved shear stress (the twinning–antitwinning asymmetry of the {112} family and non-Schmid effects). The calculation also ignores the change of orientation during deformation.

Glossary
Slip system
A combination of a slip plane and a slip direction in that plane.
Schmid factor, m
The product cosφ·cosλ: the fraction of the applied stress that is resolved as shear on a slip system.
RSS
Resolved shear stress, mσ.
CRSS
Critical resolved shear stress, at which a slip system starts to operate.
<111>
The family of body-diagonal directions of the cube, the close-packed directions of the BCC lattice.
{110}, {112}
Families of crystal planes on which slip is found in BCC metals.
Tensile axis
The direction of the applied uniaxial stress.
