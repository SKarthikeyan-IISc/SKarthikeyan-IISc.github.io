Shear Modulus of a Cubic Crystal on (001), (011) and (111) Planes

How the shear modulus depends on the plane and on the direction of shear in a cubic single crystal, from the three independent elastic constants C11, C12 and C44.

Instructions for use
  • Material: choose Cu, Nb, Na, W or V, or Custom to enter your own C11, C12 and C44 (in GPa). Custom values must describe a mechanically stable crystal, and the tool says if they do not.
  • Angle θ: drag the slider (or press Play) to turn the direction of shear within each plane. The top row shows the unit cell, the shear plane (purple), its normal (dashed purple) and the shear: the coral pair of arrows shows the material above the plane sliding along +m and the material below sliding along −m.
  • Polar plots: the radius is the shear modulus G(θ) for that direction. The solid orange curve is the part traced so far, the dotted orange curve is the full curve, and the grey circle is the Voigt average. All three plots share one scale, so they can be compared directly.
  • Save image: Save image (PNG) downloads the three views exactly as shown at the current angle, with the material, its constants and the angle written above them. Choose a light background for slides and printing.
  • Cards: give G at the current angle, the range of G in that plane, the nearest low-index direction [uvw] (indices up to 3, dashed grey in the cell) and G along it.
  • Things to try: look at the (001) and (111) planes for every material, where G does not depend on direction, and at the (011) plane, where it does. Then compare Cu (A > 1) with Nb (A < 1), and then with W, which is nearly isotropic. In the (011) plane, find the directions where G equals C44 and where it equals C′.

Physics of the problem

Shear modulus in a chosen plane and direction

A cubic crystal has three independent elastic constants, \(C_{11}\), \(C_{12}\) and \(C_{44}\), or equivalently the compliances \(S_{11}\), \(S_{12}\) and \(S_{44}\), found by inverting the stiffness matrix:

\( S_{11}=\dfrac{C_{11}+C_{12}}{(C_{11}-C_{12})(C_{11}+2C_{12})},\qquad S_{12}=\dfrac{-C_{12}}{(C_{11}-C_{12})(C_{11}+2C_{12})},\qquad S_{44}=\dfrac{1}{C_{44}} \)

If a shear stress acts on a plane with unit normal \(\mathbf n\) in a direction \(\mathbf m\) lying in that plane, the shear modulus \(G=\tau/\gamma\) for that plane and direction follows by transforming the compliance tensor to axes along \(\mathbf n\) and \(\mathbf m\):

\( \dfrac{1}{G}=S_{44}+4\left(S_{11}-S_{12}-\dfrac{S_{44}}{2}\right)\left(n_1^2m_1^2+n_2^2m_2^2+n_3^2m_3^2\right) \)

The direction dependence is carried by the single number \(\Sigma=n_1^2m_1^2+n_2^2m_2^2+n_3^2m_3^2\). For perpendicular unit vectors \(\Sigma\) lies between 0 and \(\tfrac12\), so \(G\) always lies between two values:

\( \Sigma=0:\;\; G=C_{44}\qquad\qquad \Sigma=\tfrac12:\;\; G=C'=\dfrac{C_{11}-C_{12}}{2} \)

On a {100} plane \(\mathbf n\) has a single non-zero component, so \(\Sigma=0\) for every direction in the plane: shear on any {100} plane, in any direction, gives \(C_{44}\). Shear on a {110} plane along \(\langle1\bar10\rangle\) gives \(\Sigma=\tfrac12\) and so \(C'\), and along \(\langle100\rangle\) it gives \(C_{44}\). The (011) plot is the only one of the three in which \(G\) varies with direction.

The (001) and (111) planes are isotropic in the plane

On a {111} plane \(n_i^2=\tfrac13\) for every component, so \(\Sigma=\tfrac13\sum m_i^2=\tfrac13\) for every direction in the plane. The shear modulus is therefore the same in all directions of a (111) plane, in every cubic crystal:

\( G_{(111)}=\dfrac{3\,C'\,C_{44}}{C'+2C_{44}} \)

This is why its polar plot is a circle, whatever the material, just as it is for (001), where the circle has radius \(C_{44}\).

Anisotropy and the Voigt average

The Zener ratio \(A=2C_{44}/(C_{11}-C_{12})=C_{44}/C'\) measures how far the crystal is from isotropy. For \(A=1\), \(C_{44}=C'\) and \(G\) is the same for every plane and direction. For \(A\gt1\) (Cu, Na) the stiffest shear is on the {100} planes (\(G=C_{44}\)) and the softest is {110}⟨1̄10⟩ (\(G=C'\)); for \(A\lt1\) (Nb, V) it is the other way round. The grey circle is the Voigt average, which is what a polycrystal with a uniform strain in every grain would give:

\( G_V=\dfrac{C_{11}-C_{12}+3C_{44}}{5}=\dfrac{2C'+3C_{44}}{5} \)

It is the same for every plane and direction and lies between the smallest and largest single-crystal values. It is an upper bound for a polycrystal.

Link to bonding

If the atoms interact only through central pair forces, the Cauchy relation \(C_{12}=C_{44}\) holds, and for the three cubic lattices the shear modulus averaged in this way is \(G_V=\tfrac{3}{5}K\), where \(K\) is the bulk modulus. The Cauchy pressure \(C_{12}-C_{44}\), shown above the plots, measures how far a real crystal departs from this. A positive value (Cu, Nb, W, V) points to a volume-dependent energy such as the electron gas in a metal, which stiffens the bulk modulus but not the shear modulus. A negative value points to directional bonding, as in covalent solids. Sodium, with a value close to zero, is the closest to a central-force solid among the metals shown.

The tool treats the crystal as linear elastic, with the elastic constants as the only input. They vary with temperature, and the polycrystalline moduli of real materials also depend on texture and grain shape.

Glossary
Stiffness, Cij
The elastic constants relating stress to strain. A cubic crystal has three independent values.
Compliance, Sij
The inverse relation, strain in terms of stress. The compliance matrix is the inverse of the stiffness matrix.
Shear plane and direction
The plane (with unit normal n) across which the shear acts, and the direction m in that plane along which one side slides over the other.
C′
(C11 − C12)/2, the shear modulus for shear on a {110} plane along ⟨1̄10⟩. It is also the stiffness against a tetragonal distortion at constant volume.
Zener ratio, A
2C44/(C11 − C12). It equals 1 for an isotropic crystal.
Voigt average, GV
The shear modulus of a polycrystal found by assuming that every grain has the same strain. It is an upper bound.
Cauchy relation
C12 = C44, which holds for central pair forces when each atom sits on a centre of symmetry. The Cauchy pressure is C12 − C44.
Low-index direction [uvw]
A crystallographic direction with small integer indices. A bar over an index means a negative value.
