Equilibrium Vacancy Concentration

How the competition between the energy cost and the entropy gain of vacancies sets their equilibrium concentration, and how that concentration varies with temperature.

Instructions for use
  • ΔHv: the enthalpy of formation of a vacancy, in eV.
  • T (left plot): the temperature for the free-energy curves on the left. The right plot always shows all temperatures.
  • Left plot: the enthalpy term (rising line), the entropy term −TΔS (falling curve) and their sum, the free energy ΔG, against vacancy fraction Xv. The yellow marker is the minimum of ΔG predicted by the equation in the notes. It should sit at the bottom of the ΔG curve.
  • Right plot: the equilibrium concentration against 1/T on a logarithmic axis. A straight line means Arrhenius behaviour, and its slope is −ΔHv/kB.
  • Try raising ΔHv and then T, and watch the minimum of ΔG move.

Physics of the problem

Creating a vacancy costs energy, the enthalpy of formation \(\Delta H_v\), because bonds are broken. It also adds disorder, so the entropy increases. Per atom of the crystal, for a vacancy fraction \(X_v\):

\( \Delta G = X_v\,\Delta H_v - T\,\Delta S,\qquad \Delta S = -k_B\big[(1-X_v)\ln(1-X_v) + X_v\ln X_v\big] + X_v\,\Delta S_v \)

The first term in \(\Delta S\) is the configurational entropy of distributing the vacancies over the lattice sites. The second is the vibrational entropy that each vacancy adds, because the atoms around it vibrate at lower frequencies. Here \(\Delta S_v = 3k_B\ln 1.2 \approx 0.55\,k_B\) is assumed.

The enthalpy rises linearly with \(X_v\), but the configurational entropy rises very steeply from zero (the slope of \(X\ln X\) is infinite at \(X=0\)), so \(\Delta G\) first falls and then rises. The minimum, found from \(d\Delta G/dX_v = 0\) with \(X_v \ll 1\), is the equilibrium concentration:

\( X_v^{eq} = \exp\!\left(\dfrac{\Delta S_v}{k_B}\right)\exp\!\left(-\dfrac{\Delta H_v}{k_B T}\right) \)

A crystal is therefore never perfect at any temperature above 0 K. The concentration increases exponentially with T, and a plot of \(\ln X_v^{eq}\) against \(1/T\) is a straight line with slope \(-\Delta H_v/k_B\). This is how \(\Delta H_v\) is measured experimentally. For typical metals, \(X_v\) near the melting point is of the order of \(10^{-4}\) to \(10^{-3}\).

Glossary
Vacancy
An empty lattice site in a crystal.
Xv
Fraction of lattice sites that are vacant.
ΔHv
Enthalpy of vacancy formation (eV).
ΔSv
Vibrational entropy of formation of one vacancy.
Configurational entropy
The entropy from the number of ways of arranging the vacancies on the lattice sites.
kB
Boltzmann constant, 8.617×10−5 eV/K.
Arrhenius plot
A plot of the logarithm of a thermally activated quantity against 1/T.
